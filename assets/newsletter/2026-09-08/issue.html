1 · THE FREE PICK

Under 8, Twins at Tigers (-105), 6:40 PM ET.

The total for Minnesota at Detroit opened at 8.5. It is 8 now. That part is ordinary — totals move half a run all the time.

The part that is not ordinary is the price. When the total opened at 8.5, the under cost -115. Now that the number has come down to 8, a friendlier number for under bettors, the under costs -105. It got cheaper.

Normally those two things fight each other. A number moves in your direction and the price moves against you to compensate. Here the market lowered the number and then kept leaning the same way.

Detroit's own price firmed at the same time, -129 to -137. So the market got more confident about who wins and less confident about how much gets scored.

What beats it: an early crooked inning. At 8, a single four-run frame does most of the work against you.

3 · THE NUMBER TO KNOW

-340 — What the Yankees cost tonight against Colorado. They opened -314. That is the biggest raw move on the board, and it is also the least meaningful one — see below.

4 · WHAT THE MARKET KNOWS

A move in cents is not a move in probability, and tonight's board shows why. The Yankees went -314 to -340, twenty-six cents, the biggest number on the board. Strip out what the book keeps and that is a win chance moving from about 75.8% to 77.3% — a point and a half. Philadelphia moved only thirteen cents, half as far, from -156 to -143. That is a win chance moving from about 60.9% to 58.8%, more than two points. The closer a price sits to even money, the more each cent is actually worth. Big favorites drift a lot and mean little; a coin-flip game that moves a dime has genuinely changed its mind.
