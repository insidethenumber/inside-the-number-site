1 · THE FREE PICK

Ole Miss -6.5 vs Louisville (-110), 7:30 PM ET, ABC, Nissan Stadium.

Yesterday we published Louisville +6.5. Today we are on Ole Miss -6.5. That is a real reversal, so here is the whole reasoning rather than a quiet edit.

The case for the dog was that Ole Miss went from -7 and -297 down to -6.5 and -258. Both markets moving toward the underdog together is usually the honest kind of move. That part still holds.

What changed the read is the total. It went the other way, 53.5 up to 54.5. Less margin, more expected points. A market that has genuinely soured on a favorite takes the total down with the spread; a market repricing pace does not. Add a neutral field, where none of the usual two to three home-crowd points sit inside the number, and -6.5 stops looking like a discount on the dog and starts looking like a short number on a team the market still gives a 69% chance to win outright.

What beats it: Louisville keeping this inside a score into the fourth quarter, which is exactly what Jeff Brohm does as an underdog. He is 18-6 against the spread as a five-point-or-larger dog since 2017. We know. We are taking it anyway.

OTHER CONFIDENT PLAYS

Washington State +23.5 at Washington (-105), 4:00 PM ET, NBC. Washington opened -21.5 on Monday and is -23.5. The total moved one point, 50.5 to 51.5. When the spread outruns the total, the market is adding margin rather than scoring — it is pricing in a blowout before a snap. Run the two numbers together and the implied final is Washington 37.5, Washington State 14. The Huskies need 38 in a season opener and the Cougars have to be held to two touchdowns for that to be right.

Wisconsin +21 vs Notre Dame (-108), 7:30 PM ET, NBC, Lambeau Field. The spread barely moved all week, -20.5 to -21. The Notre Dame moneyline did move, from -2400 on Monday to -1600 today. Same margin, noticeably less certainty about the outcome. When those two markets disagree, the moneyline is the one telling you something new.

4 · WHAT THE MARKET KNOWS

When the moneyline moves and the spread does not, the market is not changing how much the favorite wins by. It is changing how sure it is that the favorite wins at all. Read those two markets together and you learn more than either tells you alone.
