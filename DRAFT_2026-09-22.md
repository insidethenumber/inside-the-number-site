## SITUATION 1 — LEAD. Falcons at Packers: the total fell 3 points, the spread moved half of one.

Thu Sep 24, 8:15 PM ET, Lambeau Field, Prime Video. GB 1-1, ATL 0-2.

| Market | Opened | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Spread | GB -7.5 (-110) | **GB -7 (-105)** |
| Total | **46.5** (-110) | **43.5** (o-115 / u-105) |
| Moneyline | GB -360 / ATL +285 | GB -340 / ATL +270 |

The teaching point: the market barely changed its mind about **who** wins — half
a point off the spread, twenty cents off the moneyline. It changed its mind
hard about **how the game gets played** — three full points off the total. A
spread that holds while the total collapses is the market repricing pace and
scoring, not the winner.

Second point: -7 is a key number. The spread walked *down onto* 7 rather than
through it, and the price moved from -110 to -105 instead.

No-vig on the spread: GB -7 at -105 implies 51.2%, ATL +7 at -115 implies 53.5%.
Together 104.7% — the book's cut. Strip it and it is **GB 48.9% / ATL 51.1%**.
Worth stating plainly: with the cut removed, the market makes the *underdog*
the marginally more likely side against this number. That is what a key number
does. **Re-verify — these prices move.**

What would change it: Lambeau weather (listed Monday as intermittent clouds,
58°F) and anything that lands on either quarterback in the Thursday report.

## SITUATION 3 — Titans at Giants: the number that will not move, so the price did.

Sun Sep 27. **NYG -3, unchanged from open — but priced -120,** with TEN +3 at
+100. Total 45.5, also unchanged.

3 is the most expensive number in football to move off. When a book wants a
different balance at 3, it does not go to 3.5 — it makes you pay more for the
same number. -120 on a -3 favorite is that. Nothing here has moved; the *cost*
has.
