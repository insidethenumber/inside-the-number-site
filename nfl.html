How to read this board

Two numbers on each game are worth more than the rest. The first is movement: where the spread opened against where it sits now. A game wears a moved tag once that gap reaches half a point, because the market has revised its own opinion and told you so in public.

The second is the fair price — the two-way market with the vig stripped out. That is what the market actually thinks, as opposed to what you are charged to bet it. Work it yourself here.

Why 3 and 7 matter more than the half-point suggests

NFL games are scored in threes and sevens, so final margins pile up on those numbers far more than on the ones either side of them. That is why a spread sitting exactly on 3 or 7 is flagged above. Moving a line from 3 to 3.5 is not the same size of change as moving it from 5 to 5.5 — the first crosses a margin that actually happens a lot, and the price charged for that half-point reflects it. The same logic, in a smaller way, applies to 6, 10 and 14.

A total moving while the spread holds still is a different statement. It says the market changed its mind about pace — weather, tempo, expected possessions — without changing its mind about who wins.
