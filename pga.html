★ Our pick on this event
Rory McIlroy +850 outright
Confidence: Medium · outrights are a small-stakes market by design

This pick is mostly about the man he has to beat. Scheffler at +320 is priced like the best player alive at full strength — but he spent last week playing through hand, foot and mouth disease. His own words after the BMW: it was painful to grip the club, he couldn't practice, work out or warm up properly, withdrawal was suggested, and a week out he was still hoping the throat "will clear up the next couple days." His Thursday 72 beat three players in a 50-man field.

The format makes that matter more, not less. No cut, even start, four rounds, thirty players: season-long dominance carries nothing forward, and there's no early cushion to recover behind. If illness costs the favorite even a few points of win probability, that probability flows uphill — and the market's own board says McIlroy is where most of it lands. He's the clear second choice at nearly triple Scheffler's price, healthy, and by every account swinging well.

At +850 the bet needs 10.5% to break even against a field where the average player is 3.3%. We think the honest gap between a compromised Scheffler and a healthy McIlroy is narrower than +320 against +850 says it is.

What beats it

Scheffler recovering — he says he's improving, and even sick he played the last 54 holes at Bellerive nine under. That version of him at East Lake wins this bet nothing. And this is a golf outright: 29 ways to lose, in the most expensive market on the calendar. Size accordingly.

How to read this. "Charged as" is what the posted price implies you're paying for. Add all thirty up and you get 132.5% — the extra 32.5 points is the bookmaker's margin, spread across the field. "True" rescales everyone so the field sums to 100%: the market's honest opinion of each player's chance. Golf outrights carry the fattest margin of any mainstream market — this is a fun-sized-stakes board, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.
