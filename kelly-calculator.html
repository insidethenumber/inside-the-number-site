Why nobody actually bets full Kelly

The Kelly Criterion gives you the stake that maximizes long-run bankroll growth if your win probability is exactly right. That's the catch — real handicapping is never that precise, and full Kelly punishes a bad estimate hard. Overstate your edge by a few points and full Kelly can turn a legitimately profitable strategy into a rough ride.

Half and quarter Kelly trade some of that theoretical growth for a much smoother bankroll curve, and give you room to be wrong about your own numbers without it wrecking you. Quarter Kelly is what most disciplined bettors actually use.

If the calculator returns $0, the math is telling you something useful: at your stated probability, this bet has no edge — sizing doesn't matter if the price isn't right in the first place. Compare your number against the no-vig fair price before you ever get to staking.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Kelly Criterion in sports betting?

A formula for the mathematically optimal fraction of your bankroll to stake on a bet, given the odds offered and your true estimated win probability. It maximizes long-run bankroll growth.

How do you calculate a Kelly stake?

Kelly fraction = (bp - q) / b, where b is the decimal odds minus 1, p is your estimated win probability, and q is 1 - p. Multiply that fraction by your bankroll for the stake.

Should I bet full Kelly or fractional Kelly?

Full Kelly is mathematically optimal but swings hard on a normal losing streak. Most bettors use half or quarter Kelly, which grows the bankroll slower but with far less variance and less exposure to a bad probability estimate.
