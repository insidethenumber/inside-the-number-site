**On the photo — read this before asking for a player shot again.** The brief
asked for Coach Prime or a Colorado player. I did not use one and would not
recommend it. A photograph of an identifiable person attached to a betting pick
is a right-of-publicity problem, and college athletes add NIL on top of it. The
big betting accounts that do this are licensed, insured, or willing to eat a
takedown. We are a four-subscriber newsletter. The photo here is Canva's own
licensed stock — anonymous, no faces, no marks — which gets the same premium
feel with none of the exposure. Team **logos** are different and fine: naming
and marking the teams in a factual sports context is ordinary nominative use,
which is why Version B uses the real CU and GT marks.

Canva also invented two text elements on this one — "WEIGHS - 3.5%" and a line
reading "Ubasti Tobemazon". Both were deleted. That is the third time today a
generator has hallucinated a statistic, so the rule stands: nothing ships
without reading every string on the image.

## Number verification

Pulled from ESPN's live board and posted on at 3:23 PM CT. ESPN's API has since
stopped responding to this sandbox, so these are last verified at that time.

- Georgia Tech open: -7 / -290. Current: -6.5 / -238. Colorado +195. Total 50.5.
- **32.5% checks out exactly.** Current: GT -238 = 70.41% raw, CU +195 = 33.90%
  raw, sum 104.31%. De-vigged Colorado = 33.90 / 104.31 = **32.50%**.
- **29.8% is consistent** with Georgia Tech -290 paired against Colorado at
  about +217 at open (74.36% + 31.55% = 105.91%; 31.55 / 105.91 = 29.79%). I do
  not have Colorado's opening moneyline stored, so I could not reproduce it from
  our own data — but the figure is internally coherent and the direction of the
  move is not in doubt.

If the post goes out more than an hour after 3:23 PM CT, re-check before
attaching. Lines move, and the whole point of the post is that this one did.
