How to read this board

Hockey prices differently from football. Almost every puck line in the league is 1.5 goals, so the number itself has nothing to say — it is the price attached to it that moves. When you see a favourite at −1.5 drift from +180 to +205, the market has changed its mind without the line ever twitching.

That makes the moneyline the honest read in hockey, and the fair price the point: the two-way market with the vig stripped out. That is what the market actually thinks, as opposed to what you are charged to bet it. Work it yourself here.

Why the puck line tells you less than the price on it

Football spreads move because margins cluster on 3 and 7. Hockey has no equivalent. Goals are scarce and the puck line is pinned at 1.5 almost everywhere, so the line cannot travel the way a football spread does. What travels instead is the juice. A −1.5 favourite priced at +180 and the same team at +210 are two genuinely different markets, and only one of those numbers on the page changed.

The total behaves more like football's. When a total slides from 6.5 to 5.5 while the moneyline holds, the market has revised its view of pace — goaltending, rest, travel, who is actually starting in net — without revising who it thinks wins. Those two numbers move for different reasons, and reading them apart is most of the skill. A goalie confirmed an hour before puck drop moves a hockey total more reliably than almost anything else on the board.
